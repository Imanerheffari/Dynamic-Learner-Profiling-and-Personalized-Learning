# Dynamic Learner Profiling and Personalized Learning

## Project Overview

This project proposes an integrated **Learning Analytics framework** that considers learning as a **dynamic and evolving process** rather than as a static state.

The framework analyzes learners' academic and behavioral traces collected from a Learning Management System (LMS) in order to:

* model the evolution of learner behavior over time;
* detect learners at academic risk at an early stage;
* identify dynamic behavioral profiles;
* explain risk predictions;
* generate personalized pedagogical recommendations.

---

## Objectives

The main objectives of the project are:

* Extract and structure learners' behavioral and academic traces as temporal sequences.
* Model learner evolution using sequential Deep Learning.
* Perform early academic risk prediction from both static and temporal information.
* Identify evolving learner profiles through clustering.
* Provide interpretable risk predictions using Explainable AI.
* Generate personalized recommendations and targeted pedagogical alerts.
* Support pedagogical decision-making through data-driven insights.

---

## Dataset

The project uses the **Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD)**.

OULAD contains information about students, courses, assessments, and interactions with the Virtual Learning Environment (VLE).

The study analyzes approximately **32,593 learners** and combines:

* Demographic information
* Academic information
* Registration information
* Assessment results
* VLE interaction data

The behavioral information is aggregated at a **weekly level** to construct temporal sequences representing learner activity and performance throughout the semester.

---


## Temporal Learning Representation

Learners' weekly interactions with the VLE and their assessment-related information are organized into temporal sequences.

This representation allows the framework to capture the evolution of:

* engagement;
* activity;
* assessment behavior;
* learning performance;
* interaction with learning resources.

The temporal sequences cover the semester and are evaluated at different checkpoints:

**Week 3 → Week 7 → Week 12 → Full semester**

---

## Sequential Modeling — GRU

A **Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)** model is used to capture temporal dependencies in learner behavior.

The GRU produces two complementary outputs:

* an **academic risk score**;
* latent **learner representations (embeddings)** describing behavioral evolution.

The generated embeddings are subsequently used for dynamic learner profiling through K-Means clustering.

---

## Static Risk Prediction — XGBoost

A separate **XGBoost** model is used for early risk prediction based on static information available at the beginning of the learner's journey.

The model uses features related to:

* demographics;
* academic background;
* registration information.

This branch allows risk prediction **before sufficient behavioral data becomes available**.

Several Machine Learning models were evaluated during experimentation, including:

* Random Forest
* XGBoost
* LightGBM

XGBoost was selected as the final model for the static prediction branch based on the experimental evaluation.

---

## Dynamic Learner Profiling

**K-Means clustering** is applied to the embeddings generated by the GRU to identify groups of learners with similar behavioral patterns.

Learner profiles are analyzed at multiple temporal checkpoints:

* Week 3
* Week 7
* Week 12
* Full semester

This allows the analysis of **profile evolution and behavioral trajectories** throughout the learning process.

---

## Early Warning System

The risk scores generated by the predictive models are used to identify learners who may require early intervention.

Risk prediction is performed at different stages:

```text
Enrollment
    ↓
Week 3
    ↓
Week 7
    ↓
Week 12
    ↓
End of semester
```

This enables earlier identification of learners at risk and supports timely pedagogical intervention.

---

## Explainable AI

Explainability techniques are integrated to make risk predictions more interpretable.

The project uses:

* **SHAP** for feature importance and prediction explanations.
* **LIME** for local interpretation of individual predictions.

These techniques help identify the factors that contribute most to the predicted academic risk.

---

## Personalized Learning and Recommendations

The learner profiles and risk scores are used to support a **hybrid recommendation mechanism** combining:

* rule-based recommendations;
* profile-based personalization and collaborative filtering.

The system generates personalized recommendations for learners and targeted alerts for instructors.

Recommendations are generated at different temporal checkpoints, including **Weeks 3, 7, and 12**.

---

## Interactive Dashboard

The results of the framework — including:

* risk scores;
* learner profiles;
* recommendations;
* model explanations;

are made available through an interactive **Streamlit dashboard**.

The dashboard provides an interface for exploring learner behavior and supporting pedagogical decision-making.

The dashboard is maintained in a **separate repository**.
